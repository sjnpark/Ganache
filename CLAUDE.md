1. Project Context
Project
Ganache is a team participating in the 2026 Zero100 Singapore AI Builderthon.
The client company is Codepresso, a B2B company providing:

* AI competency assessment
* AI education
* AI transformation / AX consulting

Its customers are primarily large and mid-sized companies and public-sector organizations.
Ganache is solving:
Track 2 — Automation: Codepresso Marketing

2. Core Problem
Codepresso has a three-person marketing team:

* 1 video PD
* 2 content marketers

They manage multiple channels, including:

* YouTube
* Korean / English blog
* LinkedIn
* Newsletter
* PR / media
* Offline seminars and webinars

They produce more than 30 pieces of content per month.
The current marketing workflow consists of eight stages:

1. Idea
2. Planning
3. Writing
4. Editing
5. Publishing
6. SNS Distribution
7. Performance Collection
8. Data Accumulation & Feedback

Currently, almost all eight stages require manual work.
The goal of this project is not merely to generate content with AI.
The goal is to redesign this workflow so that the eight stages can operate as one connected AI-assisted automation system, while preserving human judgment where it is genuinely necessary.

3. Business Objective
Primary KPI
Codepresso's marketing objective is not general branding, views, or engagement alone.
The primary business outcome is:
Qualified B2B inquiries / leads generated by marketing content.
Therefore:

* Views are not sufficient evidence of success.
* Likes are not sufficient evidence of success.
* Impressions are not sufficient evidence of success.
* Content performance should ultimately be connected, wherever possible, to inquiries or leads.

When designing any feature, ask:
How does this help Codepresso create, distribute, understand, or improve content that generates B2B leads?
Do not optimize vanity metrics at the expense of this objective.

3.1 MVP Pivot (Post-Mentoring, Current & Authoritative)
Following mentor feedback, the team completed a pivot audit (2026-08-26) across all three members. This section defines the current core MVP. Where it conflicts with the original 8-stage framing elsewhere in this file (especially Section 5's end-to-end diagram and Section 14's original scope), this section takes precedence for what to build next; the earlier sections remain background and long-term vision, not the near-term target.

New Core MVP Loop
External market / competitor signals
+ Codepresso internal evidence (past content performance + qualified B2B inquiry evidence)
→ Opportunity recommendation
→ Channel selection
→ Content idea
→ Final draft / human handoff

What we are NOT building as the core MVP

* a full 8-stage automation system
* a new social media tracking tool
* a CRM
* a publishing/distribution platform (existing tools already handle this)
* a complex, general-purpose attribution system

Governing principles

* External views/likes/virality are market attention signals only — never proof of B2B conversion.
* Qualified inquiry data (`lead_events.qualified = true`) is the internal business evidence — the real ground truth.
* "Opportunity" and "Content Idea" are distinct concepts: an Opportunity is a recommended direction backed by evidence; a Content Idea is the channel-specific execution of an approved Opportunity. Do not collapse the two.
* Every recommendation (Opportunity or Content Idea) must show why it was made, with a traceable path back to its supporting external signal(s) and/or internal evidence.
* Human approval remains required before an Opportunity or Content Idea moves forward — this pivot does not remove Section 6's human-in-the-loop principle.

What this means for existing work
The existing DB schema (Section 11) already supports this direction without changes: `trends` = external signal, `lead_events`/`content_metrics` = internal evidence, `content_insights`/`insight_sources`/`idea_trends`/`idea_insights` = traceable evidence links, `ideas` = Opportunity. Existing Stage 7–8 code (performance/insight pages) is reused as the internal-evidence half of the new Opportunity/Evidence experience, not discarded.

4. Current Workflow and Pain Points
Every implementation decision should remain grounded in these actual client problems.
Stage 1 — Idea
Current state
Marketers choose topics based on:

* industry issues they happen to notice
* discussions from meetings
* individual intuition

Pain point
There is no reliable data showing which previous topics generated inquiries.
Topic selection is therefore largely intuition-based.
Desired direction
Future content ideas should be informed by:

* historical content performance
* lead / inquiry performance
* previous successful topics
* current industry issues or relevant news
* Codepresso business context

Stage 2 — Planning
Current state
Every Monday, marketers verbally decide:

* weekly topics
* channels
* schedule

Pain points

* decisions are not strongly data-driven
* meeting decisions are not consistently documented
* information remains in individual memory

Desired direction
Planning should become structured and persist as reusable data.
Where meeting transcripts or summaries are available, the system should be able to use them as planning context.
Stage 3 — Writing
Current state
Writers frequently start from a blank document.
Repeated information such as company descriptions is rewritten or copied from older files.
A blog article may take half a day to a full day.
Pain points

* repeated manual writing
* inconsistent tone
* inconsistent terminology
* the same product has previously been referred to differently across content
* existing tone-and-manner guidelines are not consistently used

Codepresso has previously tested generative AI for drafts.
The output was fluent but generic enough that it could have belonged to almost any company.
The marketer eventually rewrote the content from scratch.
Desired direction
Generated content must:

* sound like Codepresso
* use consistent terminology
* contain Codepresso-specific knowledge and perspectives
* use approved reusable company information
* avoid generic AI-style marketing copy
* follow the shared Tone & Manner Guide

Stage 4 — Editing
Current state
Proofreading, images, thumbnails, and video editing depend largely on the individual marketer.
Pain points

* quality varies by person
* visual and editorial consistency is weak
* thumbnail styles can differ from content to content

Desired direction
Editing should apply shared standards automatically wherever possible.
The system should validate:

* tone
* terminology
* structure
* factual consistency
* channel suitability

Human approval may still occur before the next stage.
Stage 5 — Publishing
Current state
Marketers manually access each channel's management tool.
They manually modify:

* title
* summary
* tags
* formatting

for each channel.
Pain point
The same underlying content requires repetitive channel-specific manual work.
Desired direction
A single approved source content item should be transformed into channel-specific outputs and prepared for publishing automatically.
If real publishing APIs or credentials are unavailable during the Builderthon, use a clearly identified demo/sandbox/mock integration rather than pretending that a real publish occurred.
Never falsely represent a mocked action as a real external API action.
Stage 6 — SNS Distribution
Current state
Published content is manually redistributed to channels such as LinkedIn.
Pain point
When the team becomes busy, secondary distribution is the first task to be skipped.
As a result, high-effort content can remain isolated in one channel.
Desired direction
Once content is approved and published, secondary distribution should be automatically prepared or triggered according to channel rules.
Stage 7 — Performance Collection
Current state
Every Friday, marketers manually open approximately four analytics tools and copy numbers into a spreadsheet.
This takes approximately two hours per week.
Pain points

* manual copying consumes time
* collected metrics are mostly reported and then forgotten
* data is not strongly connected to later decisions
* inquiries are not reliably attributed to the content that influenced them

Desired direction
The system should automatically collect or ingest performance data where technically possible.
It should organize metrics around meaningful dimensions such as:

* content
* topic
* channel
* campaign
* date/time
* relevant engagement
* inquiry / lead outcome

The system should distinguish activity metrics from actual B2B inquiry outcomes.
Stage 8 — Data Accumulation & Feedback
Current state
There is effectively no closed-loop marketing knowledge system.
A previous article generated three inquiries from enterprise education managers, but the team could not explain why it worked and therefore could not intentionally reproduce the result.
Core pain point
Performance data does not return to the next planning cycle.
Desired direction
The system must create a closed feedback loop:
Performance → Insight → Next Idea
Historical results must influence future content recommendations.
The system should make it possible to answer questions such as:
Which topics, formats, or channels have historically generated the strongest B2B inquiry outcomes?
and:
Based on previous performance, what should Codepresso create next, and why?

6. Human-in-the-Loop Principle
Codepresso does not want humans removed from judgment.
Human involvement is explicitly acceptable for two categories:
A. Review and Approval
Humans may inspect and approve outputs produced by the system.
Automation should eliminate unnecessary manual transformation work, not responsible oversight.
B. Final Performance Interpretation and Direction Setting
Humans remain responsible for interpreting overall performance and deciding strategic direction.
AI may:

* summarize
* compare
* identify patterns
* recommend
* surface anomalies

But it should not pretend that business judgment has been delegated entirely to AI.

7. Quality Is a Hard Requirement
Automation is not successful if it produces low-quality content faster.
Codepresso explicitly considers generic, machine-like B2B content harmful.
The system must prioritize:

1. Codepresso-specific relevance
2. Brand voice
3. Consistent terminology
4. Factual reliability
5. Channel appropriateness
6. B2B usefulness
7. Lead-generation relevance

Never optimize volume at the expense of these requirements.

9. Factual Safety and Approval Guardrails
Incorrect publication of the following can create serious business risk:

* customer names
* prices
* contract conditions
* confidential project information
* unsupported company claims
* unsupported numerical claims

Never fabricate these values.
If verified source data is unavailable:

* explicitly mark the value as unavailable
* use a placeholder only in clearly labeled demo data
* request or require verification before publishing

Content containing high-risk factual claims must be routed through human approval before it is treated as publish-ready.
The system should prefer traceability over confident guessing.

10. Data Principles
The database is not just storage.
It is what connects Stage 8 back to Stage 1.
The system should support traceability across concepts such as:
Idea / Topic → Content → Channel → Performance → Inquiry / Lead
Every meaningful content item should have a persistent identifier so that later performance can be connected back to its origin.
Where applicable, data should preserve:

* topic / idea
* content identity
* channel
* campaign
* content format
* approval status
* publication status
* publication time
* performance metrics
* inquiry / lead outcome
* historical insight

Do not assume final table names or columns until the shared DB schema has been confirmed.

13. Integration Contract
Each stage must define:
Input
What information does this stage receive?
Processing
What does the system do?
Output
What structured result is produced?
Storage
What should be persisted?
Next Step
Which stage consumes the output?
Human Check
Does this point require approval or review?
Whenever building a stage, explicitly think through all six.
A feature is not complete merely because its UI works.
It must fit the end-to-end workflow.
14. MVP Principle
This is an 8-day Builderthon.
See Section 3.1 for the current, authoritative MVP scope (post-pivot) — the discipline below still applies, but the specific 8-stage target it originally referred to has been superseded.
Do not overengineer.
Prefer:

* one convincing end-to-end workflow
* real traceability
* clear business logic
* a small number of reliable integrations
* a strong demo

over:

* many disconnected features
* excessive architectural complexity
* unnecessary infrastructure
* large amounts of unfinished functionality

When choosing between breadth and evidence, prefer evidence.
15. Demo-First Requirement
The final solution must be demonstrable in approximately 1–2 minutes.
Whenever implementing a feature, ask:
Can this create a visible before → process → after moment in the final demo?
The system should support a demo that makes the following understandable:

1. What manual pain existed?
2. What input enters Ganache?
3. What does Ganache automate?
4. Where does the human approve?
5. What is produced?
6. What data is captured?
7. How does that data improve the next cycle?

Avoid features whose value cannot be clearly demonstrated.

17. Development Workflow for Claude Code
For any non-trivial development request:
Step 1 — Understand
Before coding:

* read this file
* inspect relevant files
* read the DB schema if relevant
* read Tone & Manner if relevant
* identify the affected workflow stages

Step 2 — Plan
Give the user a concise plan before implementing.
The team members are beginner developers.
Explain technical decisions in simple language.
Avoid unnecessary jargon.
If jargon is required, briefly explain it.
Step 3 — Implement
Make the smallest coherent change that solves the requested problem.
Preserve existing functionality.
Reuse existing structures before creating new parallel ones.
Do not silently rewrite large parts of the project.
Step 4 — Validate
After implementation:

* check for syntax/build/runtime errors
* run relevant tests
* verify major user flows
* verify that existing functionality was not broken

If browser or Playwright-style testing is available, use it for user-facing flows.
If automated browser testing is not available, clearly state what still requires manual checking.
Step 5 — Explain
After completing the work, briefly explain:

* what changed
* which files changed
* how to test it
* any remaining limitation
* any dependency on another team member's unfinished work

20. Definition of Done
A feature should only be considered complete when:

* it addresses an identified Codepresso pain point
* it fits the correct workflow stage
* inputs and outputs are clear
* required data is persisted correctly
* it can connect to adjacent stages
* human approval rules are respected
* factual safety rules are respected
* it works in the current application
* it has been tested
* its value can be demonstrated

21. Product-Level Definition of Success
Ganache succeeds if the prototype convincingly demonstrates that Codepresso can move from:
Three marketers manually pushing content through eight disconnected steps
to:
A connected AI-driven marketing workflow in which repetitive execution is automated, human judgment remains in control, content quality remains Codepresso-specific, and every campaign creates data that improves the next one.
The ultimate loop is:
Create → Distribute → Measure → Learn → Create Better
The system is not merely a content generator.
It is a closed-loop B2B marketing workflow.
22. Final Decision Rule
When uncertain between two implementation choices, prefer the option that better satisfies these priorities, in order:

1. Solves an actual pain point stated by Codepresso
2. Preserves content quality and factual reliability
3. Strengthens the 8-step end-to-end workflow
4. Improves lead-oriented measurement and feedback
5. Reduces unnecessary human manual work
6. Creates a stronger demonstrable outcome
7. Is realistic to complete within the Builderthon timeline

Do not add AI merely because AI can be added.
Use AI where it removes a real bottleneck or improves a real decision.
